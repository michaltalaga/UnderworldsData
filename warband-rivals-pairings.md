# Warband × Rivals Deck Pairings — Warhammer Underworlds: Embergard

A many-to-many compatibility guide matching all **50 OP-legal warbands** to all **12 universal Rivals decks**. Ratings reflect how well a warband's playstyle, model count, durability and signature mechanics line up with what each deck's objectives reward.

Built from the warband warscrolls (`warbands/*/warscroll.json`), the Rivals decks (`rivals/*/deck.json`) and the Embergard core rules. Glory wins games over 3 battle rounds; the right Rivals deck turns a warband's natural behaviour into Glory.

## Rating legend

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ★★★ | **Signature fit** — the warband's core mechanics directly power the deck's win condition |
| ★★ | **Good fit** — plays to the warband's strengths, a solid competitive choice |
| ★ | **Workable** — functions, but no real synergy; situational |
| *(blank)* | **Weak / avoid** — the deck pulls against what the warband wants to do |

## By deck — recommended warbands

Each deck's strongest pairings, with why. ★★★ = signature, ★★ = strong runners-up.

### NP — Nexus of Power (treasure board-control)
Wants 5+ durable melee bodies that can spread across the map and brawl while parked on treasure; the *covetous* +1 Range plot loves short-range fighters. Avoid 3-model elites and pure backline shooters.
- **★★★ Khagra's Ravagers** — desecrating treasure tokens *is* their engine; every objective rewarding treasure proximity feeds them.
- **★★★ Grandfather's Gardeners** — near-unkillable treasure-tracker grind; out-holds anyone on objectives.
- **★★★ The Blood of the Bull** — Delve treasure → daemonforge dice → combat power; treasure becomes fuel.
- **★★★ Borgit's Beastgrabbaz** — Delve-Inspire plus a leader-anchored cluster that holds and fights on treasure.
- **★★★ Grinkrak's Looncourt** — treasure-holding quests + 7 bodies to cover multiple tokens.
- **★★★ The Starblood Stalkers** — Inspire literally needs 3+ treasure held; perfectly on-theme.
- ★★ Spiteclaw's Swarm, Kamandora's Blades, Sepulchral Guard, Kainan's Reapers, Da Kunnin' Krew, Thanatek's Tithe, Jaws of Itzl, Thundrik's Profiteers.

### HG — Hunting Grounds (defensive home turf)
Wants a tanky warband with a powerful leader that lures enemies into friendly territory and counter-punches. Avoid fragile glass-cannons that must invade, and warbands with a weak leader.
- **★★★ Mollog's Mob** — a self-healing monster leader that squats at home and punishes anyone who approaches.
- **★★★ Grandfather's Gardeners** — the ultimate hold-and-endure home-turf warband.
- **★★★ The Grymwatch** — Save re-rolls *in friendly territory* are tailor-made for defending home.
- **★★★ Kainan's Reapers** — disciplined, tanky Mortek line that refuses to move off home ground.
- **★★★ The Sons of Velmorn** — king-centric shieldwall with a death-curse that punishes attackers.
- **★★★ Morgok's Krushas** — three tough Brutes that win the drawn-out home fight.
- **★★★ The Wurmspat** — durable, slows invaders with bile, ideal counter-punchers.
- **★★★ Hrothgorn's Mantrappers** — literally lay traps in your territory; leader-kill Inspire.
- **★★★ Ironsoul's Condemners** — can't be driven back, Bulwark defense; immovable home holders.
- **★★★ Jaws of Itzl** — Obstinate makes the Saurus an immovable home anchor.
- **★★★ Kurnoth's Heralds** — Flying + anti-Flank *in friendly territory*; strike out from a defended home.

### DY — Deadly Synergy (cluster & gang-attack)
Wants 4-6 mid-tier fighters that pack tight and surround one enemy at a time; the *united* plot Flanks anyone adjacent to your cluster. Avoid lone-wolf elites, spread skirmishers and spacing-hungry shooters.
- **★★★ Zarbag's Gitz** — the densest swarm in the game; Gang Up stacks dice per adjacent Grot.
- **★★★ Da Kunnin' Krew** — best Flank/Surround payoff; grots count double for surrounding.
- **★★★ Kamandora's Blades** — converge and gang a single "worthy skull" every round.
- **★★★ Kainan's Reapers** — lockstep formation that fights in permanent adjacency.
- **★★★ The Exiled Dead** — adjacency grants Attack re-rolls; a packed puppet-line.
- **★★★ The Sons of Velmorn** — Inspire triggers on all fighters adjacent to an attacked target.
- **★★★ The Skinnerkin** — haunch tokens flow from slaying Flanked/Surrounded foes.
- **★★★ Thanatek's Tithe** — Retainer-adjacency formation extends reach and clusters.
- **★★★ Spiteclaw's Swarm** — Scheming Pack rewards Flanked/Surrounded targets; raises keep the cluster full.
- **★★★ Sepulchral Guard / Thorns of the Briar Queen** — 7-body swarms that auto-Surround.
- **★★★ Borgit's Beastgrabbaz** — minions cluster on the leader and gang the marked target.

### RG — Raging Slayers (all-in charging berserkers)
Wants a small (3-5), fast, all-melee warband — ideally with a killer leader — that throws *every* fighter into enemy territory. Avoid 6+ hordes (the "each fighter" objectives get hard), shooters and holders.
- **★★★ Gorechosen of Dromm** — three Khorne berserkers; the deck is their warscroll in card form.
- **★★★ Morgok's Krushas** — three durable chargers that thrive enraged.
- **★★★ The Crimson Court** — hunger *forces* constant attacking; fast bloodthirsty vampires.
- **★★★ Kamandora's Blades** — Khorne pursuers with free pushes toward the kill.
- **★★★ Skittershank's Clawpack** — fast assassins that charge in to behead the leader.
- **★★★ Rippa's Snarlfangs** — the fastest chargers in the game with a double-attack.
- **★★★ The Headsmen's Curse** — elite executioners that teleport in and kill.
- **★★★ Mollog's Mob** — leader-driven kills; "slay with your leader" objectives.
- **★★★ Gnarlspirit Pack** — aggressive elites that bank Move/Attack buffs.
- **★★★ Elathain's Soulraid** — front-loads a brutal round-2 charge spike.
- **★★★ The Shadeborn** — hyper-mobile assassins that strike enraged.
- **★★★ Knives of the Crone** — attacks fuel the prophecy engine; more charges = more buffs.
- **★★★ Kurnoth's Heralds** — three hard-hitting Sylvaneth with burst strikes.

### RS — Realmstone Raiders (attack-frequency card engine)
Wants width — many fighters landing many successful melee Attacks per round (Realmstone Raid wants 3 different attackers) and high-Bounty targets. Avoid low-activation elites and pure shooters.
- **★★★ Zarbag's Gitz** — nine attackers; raid volume incarnate.
- **★★★ Sepulchral Guard / Thorns / The Exiled Dead** — 7-body lines (Thorns/Exiled even re-raise) for relentless attacks.
- **★★★ The Grymwatch** — seven ghouls grinding out hits, raising more.
- **★★★ Grinkrak's Looncourt** — seven Loonknights making attacks every turn.
- **★★★ Daggok's Stab-ladz** — free "Krule Stab" chip attacks are *extra* raid triggers.
- ★★ Skittershank's, Kamandora's, Da Kunnin', Kainan's, The Crimson Court, Headsmen's, Skinnerkin, Borgit's, Rippa's, Starblood, Elathain's, Knives.

### EK — Edge of the Knife (low-health swarm)
Wants numerous **Health-2-or-less** bodies that are *tempered* from turn one, flooding enemy territory and trading. Avoid elite high-Health warbands and 3-model elites.
- **★★★ Zarbag's Gitz** — nine low-Health grots; tempered before the first die is rolled.
- **★★★ Spiteclaw's Swarm / Sepulchral Guard / Thorns / The Exiled Dead** — cheap fragile swarms that *want* to trade and recur.
- **★★★ Skittershank's Clawpack** — low-armour assassins, fragile and fast.
- **★★★ Zikkit's Tunnelpack** — literally suicide-bombs its own fighters; self-sacrifice is upside here.
- **★★★ Ephilim's Pandaemonium** — fragile minions farmed for value.
- **★★★ The Dread Pageant** — Inspire and payoffs trigger on *being damaged* — tempered synergy.
- **★★★ The Starblood Stalkers** — low-Health Skinks tempered immediately.
- **★★★ Hexbane's Hunters** — six fragile bodies whose Inspire keys off their own deaths.
- **★★★ Knives of the Crone** — squishy, fast Khainites happy to be on the edge.

### RF — Reckless Fury (Charge-token aggression)
Wants relentless chargers that can generate Charge tokens every round and hunt high-Bounty/leader targets; fast and at least semi-durable. Avoid passive holders, shooters and ultra-fragile builds.
- **★★★ The Dread Pageant, Skittershank's, Gorechosen, Gnarlspirit, The Crimson Court, Kamandora's, Headsmen's Curse, Mollog's Mob, Rippa's, Morgok's, Elathain's Soulraid, The Shadeborn, Knives of the Crone** — all fast, charge-hungry killers that want to slam enemy territory and behead high-value targets.
- **★★★ Zikkit's Tunnelpack** — extreme mobility and extra Moves to charge from anywhere.
- ★★ Spiteclaw's, Thorns, Grymwatch, Da Kunnin', Grinkrak's, Daggok's, Farstriders, Starblood, Emberwatch, Jaws of Itzl, Kurnoth's Heralds.

### WR — Wrack and Ruin (attrition & chip damage)
Wants tough fighters that survive dispersed and damaged, win on chip/non-attack damage, and tolerate hurting themselves. Avoid tight swarms (objectives want *no* adjacency) and alpha-strike fragile builds.
- **★★★ Grandfather's Gardeners** — caps incoming damage, Inspires *on becoming vulnerable*; the perfect grind body.
- **★★★ The Wurmspat** — Disgustingly Resilient + bile chip damage; thrives sitting damaged.
- **★★★ The Dread Pageant** — its whole kit pays off *while damaged*.
- **★★★ Morgok's Krushas** — tough Brutes built for the long slog.
- **★★★ Daggok's Stab-ladz** — chip-damages many enemies; damage-spread is their game.
- **★★★ The Thricefold Discord** — control/chip-damage daemons that grind tempo.
- **★★★ Ylthari's Guardians** — get *stronger* as their own fighters die.
- **★★★ Xandire's Truthseekers** — death-reaction payoffs reward attrition.
- **★★★ The Emberwatch** — actively *want* to spread out, exactly as the deck asks.

### BL — Blazing Assault (pure aggro)
The most warband-agnostic deck — point any hard-hitting, accurate killer at the enemy and slay. Best with elite damage; weak with passive holders and low-damage swarms that can't reliably kill.
- **★★★ Gorechosen, Gnarlspirit, Kamandora's, Skittershank's, The Crimson Court, Headsmen's Curse, Mollog's Mob, Rippa's, Morgok's, The Skinnerkin, Elathain's, The Shadeborn, Knives of the Crone, Kurnoth's Heralds** — straightforward elite killers.
- **★★★ Myari's Purifiers** — re-roll engine makes its kills reliable (good for the dice-quality objectives).
- **★★★ Jaws of Itzl** — Savage Mauling snowballs into a leader-nuke.

### ES — Emberstone Sentinels (pure objective holding)
Wants durable mid-count fighters that grab treasure and refuse to die — essentially zero kill requirement. Avoid fragile glass-cannons and small warbands that can't cover multiple tokens.
- **★★★ Grandfather's Gardeners** — out-holds everything; treasure even advances their tracker.
- **★★★ Khagra's Ravagers** — tough treasure-holders whose engine runs on contested tokens.
- **★★★ Kainan's Reapers** — tanky Mortek that anchor objectives.
- **★★★ Thanatek's Tithe** — durable constructs that sit and hold.
- **★★★ Grinkrak's Looncourt** — 7 bodies + treasure quests cover several tokens.
- **★★★ The Starblood Stalkers** — Inspire wants 3+ treasure; built to occupy.
- **★★★ Thundrik's Profiteers** — "This is Mine" makes a treasure-carrier push-immune.
- **★★★ Jaws of Itzl** — an immovable Saurus on a token is nearly impossible to shift.

### PL — Pillage and Plunder (mobile treasure denial)
Wants fast fighters that dive into enemy territory to Delve and strip treasure off the board. Avoid slow/static warbands, tight clusters and pure killers with no objective plan.
- **★★★ The Dread Pageant** — fast, and already wants to deny enemy treasure-holders.
- **★★★ Zondara's Gravebreakers** — Delving *is* their engine (Exhume/Unearth).
- **★★★ The Blood of the Bull** — Delve treasure to power daemonforge dice.
- **★★★ Borgit's Beastgrabbaz** — Inspire on Delve; built to interact with tokens.
- **★★★ The Starblood Stalkers** — fast, with Kixi-Taka's feature-token control.
- **★★★ The Farstriders** — elite speedsters that dive deep into enemy ground.
- **★★★ The Shadeborn** — cover-teleporting assassins reach any token.
- **★★★ The Emberwatch** — teleport mobility + a spread-out playstyle that fits denial.

### CC — Countdown to Cataclysm (escalating attrition)
Wants resilient warbands that grind the game long, willingly trade/recur fighters (each slain friendly advances the doom clock), and ideally stack upgrades on an elite. Avoid fast glass-cannons wanting a quick game.
- **★★★ Sepulchral Guard, The Exiled Dead, The Grymwatch, Sons of Velmorn, Zondara's, Thanatek's Tithe, Ephilim's Pandaemonium** — raise/recursion warbands that feed the clock and never run out of bodies.
- **★★★ Grandfather's Gardeners, The Wurmspat, Morgok's Krushas, Kainan's Reapers** — attrition-proof bricks that thrive late.
- **★★★ Ylthari's Guardians, Xandire's Truthseekers, Hexbane's Hunters** — uniquely *benefit* from losing fighters.
- **★★★ Daggok's Stab-ladz** — damage-over-time + card draw scale into the long game.

## How to read the pairings (method notes)

The ratings weigh four things, in roughly this order:

1. **Does the deck's *Plot keyword* match the warband?** Six decks have a build-around Plot — *covetous* (NP), *united* (DY), *enraged* (RG), *raid* (RS), *tempered* (EK), *Cataclysm* (CC). A warband that naturally satisfies the keyword (e.g. a Health-2 swarm for *tempered*, a 9-model cluster for *united*) gets a big boost.
2. **Do the deck's *scoring objectives* match the warband's behaviour?** Kill decks want killers; hold decks want tanky bodies; swarm/adjacency decks want clusters; mobility decks want speed.
3. **Model count & durability vs the objective demands.** "Each friendly fighter…" objectives favour 3-5 fighters; "X fighters in enemy territory" favour 5-7+; "survive/hold" favours tough bodies; fragile glass-cannons clash with endure decks but shine in trade-happy ones.
4. **Inspire trigger alignment.** A warband whose Inspire fires on the same actions the deck rewards (kills, holding treasure, taking damage, charging) effectively scores and powers up at once.

The six **no-Plot decks** (HG, RF, WR, BL, ES, PL) are more about raw objective shape than a keyword, so their best fits track playstyle directly: HG/ES want defensive holders, RF/BL want aggressors, WR wants grinders, PL wants mobile raiders.

> All 50 warbands here are OP-legal (per `warbands/index.json`); the 14 non-OP-legal warbands (e.g. Garrek's Reavers, Steelheart's Champions) are excluded. Ratings are strategic guidance, not tournament results — any warband can play any universal Rivals deck.
